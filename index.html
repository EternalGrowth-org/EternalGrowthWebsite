The idea

We are high school students who teach the kids a few grades behind us. We remember what made each idea click, because it happened to us not long ago. We run the club ourselves, and what it raises goes back into Long Island.

Just ahead

A tutor only a few grades older still remembers learning the same unit, and how it felt.

Run by students

Matching, outreach and the books are handled by officers who are in school themselves.

Given back

What the club raises is donated to local organizations its treasurers choose.

For every stage.

From a first times table to a college essay, there is a tutor who has just been where your student is.

Grades K–5

Elementary school

Number sense and arithmetic, reading for meaning, first science projects, and the habits that make homework easier.

Grades 6–8

Middle school

Algebra and geometry, essay structure, first programs in Python, lab reports, and a first look at competition math.

Grades 9–12

High school

Precalculus and calculus, SAT and ACT math, AMC and USACO preparation, close reading for English classes, and college planning.

From request to progress.

Four steps. The first one takes a minute.

  1. 01

    Tell us what you need

    The subject, the grade, and what is getting in the way. One short form, at the end of this page.

  2. 02

    We match a tutor

    Someone who knows the subject well and suits the way your student learns.

  3. 03

    Together they make a plan

    Goals for the term, at a pace that follows what the school is teaching.

  4. 04

    Then it grows

    Regular sessions, steady progress, and a student who starts asking the next question.

One-on-oneSmall groupsOnlineLong Island

Three ways to learn.

One-on-one

One tutor and one student for the whole session. The best fit for catching up, or racing ahead.

Small groups

A few students working through the same unit together, learning from the tutor and from each other.

Online

Video sessions from anywhere on Long Island, for the weeks when the calendar is already full.
